Achille, about 1 minute. We want to compare entities, so we project. We build a matrix B with the entities as rows, the connectors as columns, and a 1 when they are linked. The product B times B transposed gives, for every pair of entities, the number of connectors they share: that is the edge weight. In the small example, E1 and E2 share Marie, E2 and E3 share Cabinet X, and E4 is isolated. A projection has a cost: a connector linked to d entities creates d times d minus 1 over 2 edges, so one intermediary with 7,016 entities would add about 24.6 million edges. We therefore ignore the hubs when we link entities: officers above 500 entities, intermediaries and addresses above 50. The projection has 213,634 nodes and 1,238,967 weighted edges. We tested the cap from 10 to 500: the share of isolated entities stays between 31 and 72 percent, so it is a property of the data.
