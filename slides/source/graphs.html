Achille, about 1 minute 40. Step 1: every row has a sourceID, the leak it comes from, so one line of pandas keeps the Panama Papers. We keep the three link types that attach an entity to the network and only the links whose two ends are in our subset. Step 2: officers, intermediaries and addresses are connectors. We put them in one table of connector and entity pairs, remove duplicates and add each pair as an edge. The graph is bipartite, with entities on one side and connectors on the other: 468,623 nodes and 511,687 edges, undirected and simple. Step 3: to compare entities, we project. We build a matrix B with entities as rows and connectors as columns, and a 1 when they are linked. The product B times B transposed gives, for each pair of entities, the number of connectors they share: that is the edge weight. In the small example, E1 and E2 share Marie, E2 and E3 share Cabinet X, and E4 is isolated. A projection has a cost: a connector linked to d entities creates d times d minus 1 over 2 edges, so one intermediary with 7,016 entities would add 24.6 million edges. We ignore the hubs: officers above 500 entities, intermediaries and addresses above 50. Result: 213,634 nodes and 1,238,967 weighted edges. We tested caps from 10 to 500: the share of isolated entities stays between 31 and 72 percent, so it is a property of the data.
